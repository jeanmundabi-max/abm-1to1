# Hooks: how the card and the first line are found

_Part of the `abm-1to1` skill. Read before `08-the-creative.md` and before the council is
briefed. Added 2026-09-11 from Jean's review of the ElevenLabs dry run: "the hook can be
better, similar to what you did for Revolut. You should have a copywriting hooks SOP."_

## The standard

The three Revolut cards, which Jean called *magnifiques*:

| Account | Hook | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Renishaw | **Out of Tolerance** | They make the instruments that measure drift. The card is one of their own scales, and one tick, in Revolut's blue, sits outside the band. The number is on a device the reader uses every day |
| Currys | **Wall of Screens** | A wall of dark televisions, their own shop floor. The lit ones glow Revolut's blue and spell the number |
| Hays | **Placement Board** | Person marks, the image of headcount. Grey is placed, blue is what nobody placed |

The pattern, and it is the whole SOP: **an image from inside the target's own visual world,
on which the client's colour marks the one element that carries the meaning, with the
target's own number as the biggest thing on it.** A reader recognises the object before she
reads a word. That recognition is what stops the scroll; the number is what earns the click.

## What failed on ElevenLabs, so it is never repeated

*"A flat line is a queue"* over an audio waveform, the account's number above it. Three faults:

1. **Wrong world.** A waveform is the vendor's world (audio). The buyer's world is a contact
   centre: a wallboard, a queue display, a headset, an IVR menu, a hold-music loop.
2. **A line of copy as the image.** The hook was a sentence, and a sentence is a caption, not
   a picture. The Revolut cards have almost no words.
3. **One idea for six accounts.** The same card six times with a different number is a
   template, and a template reads as an ad for anything.

## The method, in order

1. **Name the buyer's visual world.** Write down five objects the person in the seat looks at
   every day. For a contact-centre director: the wallboard (calls waiting, longest wait, agents
   available), the queue position announcement, the IVR menu, the after-call work timer, the
   complaints table the regulator prints. For a treasurer: the dealing screen, the rate ticker,
   the reconciliation. For a CFO: the ledger, the P&L line, the audit note.
2. **Find the object that already shows their number.** The wallboard shows 3:33. The
   regulator's table shows the star rating. The IVR says "you are number 47 in the queue". Pick
   the object where the published figure would naturally appear.
3. **Put the client's colour on the one element that carries the meaning.** On the wallboard,
   every tile is grey except one, in the client's colour, that reads what changes. Never the
   background, never the number itself.
4. **The number is the biggest thing on the card.** Ivan: the headline IS the creative. Here
   the number is the headline.
5. **Choose the line with one of Ivan's six formulas** (Feeling, Conversation, Contrast, Shame,
   Stat Interrupt, Pain + Outcome) and keep it to one short line. It sits under the image, not
   on it.
6. **The standalone test.** Cover the copy. Does the picture alone stop a scroll and say who it
   is for? If the answer needs the words, start again.
7. **Three concepts per account, one world each, then the council picks.** Never one. Never the
   same object for two accounts.
8. **The card is the film paused at its drop.** The film is the card in motion, so the click
   resolves the still into the same moving image.

## Worked, for the ElevenLabs rebuild

| Account | Their world | The object | The client's colour on |
|---|---|---|---|
| BT | the contact-centre wallboard | a grey wallboard with only the tiles Ofcom printed: AVERAGE WAIT 3:33 · HUNG UP 14% | one tile, ANSWERED 0:01 |
| Monzo | the in-app support thread | a chat thread with the "we'll get back to you" bubble, 64% of them past three days | the one reply that arrives in a second |
| British Gas | the complaints table British Gas itself publishes | their own quarterly table, 120,156, as they print it | the row that is not there yet: next quarter |
| HMRC | the queue announcement | the printed script of the hold message, with HMRC's own 11:35 as the wait on it | the line that replaces it: "Hello." |
| Admiral | the renewal letter | the letter with the phone number and the hours on it | the hours, 24, in colour |
| easyJet | the departure board | a departures board at 21:04 with CANCELLED, and the help line's hours printed under it, 8am to 8pm | the one line that answers at 21:04 |

Each of these is a picture first. The number is on the object. The line under it comes last.

**Only published numbers go on the object.** A wallboard has tiles for calls waiting and agents free; if the file does not print them, those tiles are not drawn. A made-up tile, even captioned as illustrative, is a made-up number on a card that argues it invents nothing. First attempt at the BT wallboard did exactly that and was redrawn with two tiles. Rendered example: `reference/hook-example-bt-wallboard.png`.

## Reject on sight

A flat brand-coloured rectangle with a number on it. A quoted sentence from a filing as the
hook. A countdown. A generic photograph. Any card that could be an ad for anything. Any card
where the client's colour is background paint.
