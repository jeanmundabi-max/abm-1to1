# Cold traffic, and the offer this play forgot it needed

**Read this before writing a single line of page or ad copy.**

---

## Why this file exists

On 2026-09-03, a grep across this entire skill for "cold traffic", "Stranger Test" and "new
money" returned **nothing**.

This skill builds landing pages and LinkedIn ads aimed at people who have never heard of the
client, and it contained no cold-traffic doctrine at all. The consequence was not subtle. The
Revolut run produced three pages of beautifully evidenced diagnosis, every figure traced to an
audited filing, and **not one of them could be said yes to.** The operator was told three times
that the creative read flat before anyone thought to check the temperature of the offer
underneath it.

The copy was never the problem. **The offer was warm and no copy can save a warm offer on cold
traffic.**

---

## The master question

> **"Can a stranger say yes to this without trusting me, understanding my system in full, or
> believing that I'm any good?"**

If the answer is no at any point, this is a warm-traffic offer. Rewriting the words will not
change that. The offer itself has to be rebuilt.

They will still have questions. That is fine. The questions get answered in the follow-up, the
page, the call. **The ad and the page have one job: get a hand raised.**

---

## The four properties, and the test that catches the trap

| Property | What it means | Fails as |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific** | An exact outcome. A number, a deliverable, a figure | "better visibility", "the one question" |
| **Finite** | A stated time frame and scope | open-ended, "when you're ready" |
| **Definite** | It produces **new money or new opportunity** | an improvement on something they already have |
| **Safe** | They can say yes without trusting you. **Ask what the yes costs in TRUST, not only in money and time** | "book a 30 minute call", and equally **"send us your bank statements"**, which is free and fast and needs more trust than a call |

**Definite is the one that catches people, including this skill.** An offer can carry a number
and still be warm, because the number is framed as a saving:

- Save 20% on shipping → **WARM**
- Save £10k to £50k of overhead → **WARM**
- Exchange at the interbank rate instead of your bank's spread → **WARM.** This was the Revolut
  offer, and it is the same shape with a different noun
- Diagnose where your funnel breaks → **WARM**, and worse, it requires trusting the diagnosis

- Five qualified calls in 30 days → **COLD**
- £120k of net margin in five months → **COLD**
- A figure that does not exist anywhere today, produced in two weeks → **COLD**, because it is
  new information rather than a saving

### The reframe, and it works on almost every improvement

Ask: **what does this improvement actually facilitate?**

Faster shipping facilitates faster inventory turnover, which is sales velocity, which is new
money. On the Revolut run the reframe came out of the page's own best sentence, the question in
the qualify section: *"can anyone in the building see it as a number rather than as a rate?"*
Nobody can. So producing that number is not an improvement, it is the creation of something that
did not exist, and that is a cold offer.

## Three traps this play walks into, all found on live builds

### 1. The diagnosis is not the offer

The strongest thing this skill does is find a dated, audited, named sentence in an account's own
filings and put it on a page built for that one company. It is genuinely good and it is **not an
offer**. A page can prove it understands the reader perfectly and still give them nothing to say
yes to. Fourteen sections of evidence ending in "speak to sales" is a document, not a funnel.

### 2. The specimen label kills the ask

Layer 2 says: never write the client's commercial offer. Cold traffic says: the page must carry
a Specific, Finite, Definite, Safe offer. **Both cannot be satisfied by the operator**, and the
first attempt to satisfy both produced a page stamped three times with *"SPECIMEN OFFER, the
client sets the final terms"*.

That is honest and it is dead. A cold reader who is told the advertiser has not agreed to this
cannot act on it.

**The resolution, and it is now the rule.** On a **demonstration**, the operator **infers** a
cold offer from what the client already publishes, presents it on the page **as a real offer**,
and discloses the inference in the **footer disclaimer and the handover**, where the audience is
the operator and the client rather than the buyer. On a **live engagement** the client supplies
the offer, and that is a blocking intake question, not a build-day discovery.

### 3. Nothing on the page can receive a yes

The Revolut pages carried a genuine cold offer, "send one month of statements", and the button
linked to the client's generic homepage. **No form, no upload, no calendar, no address,
anywhere on the page.** The button did not do what it said.

**A cold page needs a commitment device in the first viewport**, repeated at the close, and it
has to match the ask. A calendar is not a match for "send us a statement", because "book a
meeting" is the warm ask the offer was rebuilt to remove.

## Four ways a cold offer dies that copy cannot reach

Found 2026-09-08 by running the live Hays page through an adversarial council, three of whose
voices landed on the same faults without speaking to each other. Every one of these is upstream
of the words, so no rewrite touches them.

**1. The hook proves a problem the offer cannot solve.** The page opened on £23.1m off net fees
and £14.9m back, and said so itself: those are TRANSLATION effects, foreign results retranslated
into sterling. They would be identical if the company paid zero spread on every conversion. The
offer measures transaction cost. The one reader who knows that distinction cold is the Group
Treasurer, who is the target.
**Check: does the evidence you opened with actually imply the problem you are selling into?**
A filed number that is merely adjacent to the offer is worse than no number, because it invites
the most qualified reader to do the arithmetic and find the gap.

**2. The deliverable cannot be produced from the input requested.** The page promised the rate
matched to interbank "for that day and that hour". A bank statement export carries a value date,
not an execution timestamp, and intraday range on a major pair routinely exceeds the spread being
measured.
**Check: open the input you are asking for and confirm the output can be built from it.** If it
needs a dealing blotter, ask for a blotter and say so.

**3. The ask dies in governance, not in copy.** A recruiter's statements carry named contractor
payments, so personal data: lawful basis, a data processing agreement, retention, a named
controller, a sub-processor list, probably a DPIA, plus a vendor security assessment. An FAQ line
saying "one analyst reads it and the file is deleted" is an assertion, not an instrument.
**Check: what has to be TRUE INSIDE THEIR ORGANISATION for a yes to be legal?** If the answer
involves three other functions, that route is not the primary route however good the copy is.
Lead with the route that needs nobody else, and earn the data later.

**4. The offer assumes a way of working nobody asked about.** A listed treasury usually deals
over a multi-bank platform which stamps every fill against a mid and produces this exact report
as a standing output. Transaction cost analysis on FX is a mature product. If they are on a
platform the report is a duplicate; if they deal by phone and portal it is genuinely new.
**Check: is there ONE question that decides whether your deliverable is new information or a
duplicate?** If there is, the page has to ask it, and an offer that does not ask it is telling
half its readers they are not doing their job.

## Where this sits in the sequence

1. **This file.** Is the offer cold at all?
2. `offer-temperature-check` skill. Layer 1 the offer, Layer 2 the framing. **Layer 1 is binary
   and if it fails, stop. Do not evaluate the copy.**
3. [the upstream ad-copywriting guide](https://github.com/swan-gtm/gtm-skills/blob/main/skills/ivan-falco/ad-copywriting/SKILL.md). Now that there is something to say, how to say it on a card.
4. `cold-email-grader`, if the second channel is running.

**Never grade copy quality on an offer that has not passed Layer 1.** That is the mistake this
run made for three days.

---

## The gate

An account's page is not ready to publish until all five are true:

- [ ] The offer produces new money or new information, not an improvement
- [ ] A stranger can picture the result in three seconds
- [ ] A time frame and a scope are stated
- [ ] The risk is handled, and the offer is allowed to conclude "not worth it"
- [ ] **Something on the page receives the yes**, and it matches the words on the button

## Guardrail

| Failure, really happened | Fix |
|---|---|
| Three pages of audited evidence ending in "speak to sales". Every figure true, nothing actionable | The diagnosis is not the offer. Run Layer 1 before the page is designed, not after it ships |
| Three rounds of ad creative rejected as flat while the offer underneath was warm | Copy quality is irrelevant until Layer 1 passes. Check the temperature first |
| A page stamped "SPECIMEN OFFER" three times, which made it honest and unbookable | On a demo, infer the offer and disclose the inference in the footer, never on the CTA |
| A button reading "Send one month of statements" that linked to the client's homepage | A commitment device on the page, in the first viewport, matching the ask |
| The offer question was never asked at intake, so it was discovered in week two | Blocking question 6 in `../sops/01-onboarding.md` |
| The skill contained no cold-traffic doctrine at all while building cold-traffic assets | This file |
| **A page cloned from another account kept the first account's citation in its footer.** Two of three pages told a cold reader their figures came from a document belonging to a different company | **Grep every cloned page for the source account's name, its document and its dates before publishing.** A substitution that misses a citation produces a lie with a date on it, which is the one error this play cannot survive |
| **One form, two offers, one button.** The file field said "optional" and the microcopy offered a screen share instead, while the button said "Send one month of statements". A reader taking the safe route had to press a button claiming something they had not chosen | **The button label follows the route the reader picked.** If the close carries two ways in, make the choice explicit and swap the label with it. An optional field does not fix a button that lies |
| **The safe route demoted to an "Or" paragraph.** For a Group Treasurer, uploading statements to an unknown page costs more trust than a call, so the screen share was the route half the committee would take, set in grey at 0.95rem after the real ask | Give the low-trust route **equal weight beside the primary one**, not underneath it. Trust cost, not price, decides which route a senior finance seat takes |
| **Finite went missing exactly where it mattered.** "Fourteen days" appeared four times up the page and nowhere in the close, so the point of commitment had no when | State the time frame **at the ask**, and anchor it to arithmetic the reader can check: "fourteen days from the file landing, not from a kickoff call. A file sent today returns the figure on <date>" |
| **A date written into a static page was wrong the next morning.** The close anchored the fourteen days with "a file sent today puts the figure on the desk on 17 September". Correct on the day it shipped, off by one the following morning, and wronger every night after | **Compute the date in the page**, not in the build. An instruction to refresh it is not a mechanism, and a decaying date sitting beside audited figures discredits the audited figures |
| **The inference disclosure sat under the button, in bold.** Honest, required, and functionally the specimen label again in smaller type. The last thing read before clicking was a sentence saying the advertiser has not agreed this | Keep the disclosure, put real distance between it and the CTA, and set it as provenance rather than a retraction. **Adjacency is the defect, not the sentence** |
| **Nothing said what happens to the file.** The first objection from a treasury seat is "you want access to our banking", and the page never separated a statement export from access | Answer it in the route copy: an export, not access. No logins, no read only connection. Who reads it, and when it is deleted |
| An internal research note faced the buyer: "the client publishes no customer film, so it is not used here as proof" | True, useful, and it belongs in the handover. On the page it tells a cold reader we looked for proof and found none |
