Why nobody has ever defended this number

A fee has to be argued for. A spread does not.

When your bank converts sterling into euros it does not charge you a fee and then convert. It quotes you a rate. Sitting inside that rate is the difference between what you were given and the interbank rate, the rate banks use between themselves at that moment. That difference is the spread, and it is measured in basis points.

Here is why it never comes up. A fee arrives as a line on a statement, so somebody has to approve it, and somebody eventually asks whether it is competitive. A spread arrives as a rate. It is never a line, so it is never approved, never questioned and never reported. It does not appear in your accounts, your management pack or your auditor's file. There is nothing to find.

Translational

Your German net fees retranslated into sterling at a different rate. This is the £23.1m and the £14.9m. It is a reporting movement and it costs no cash.

Transactional

Actual money crossing an actual currency. Paying a contractor in euros, receiving a advertiser payment in Australian dollars. This one costs cash, every time, and it is the one nobody measures.

Constant currency reporting does not touch this. Reporting like for like tells your board how much the currency moved the numbers. It changes none of the cash that left on the transactional side. Your preliminary report already separates the two properly. The gap is that only one of them has ever been measured.

Why the timestamp matters more than anything else here. Comparing an executed rate to a daily average is the usual way this is done and it is wrong in both directions: it flatters a bad conversion made in a quiet hour and it punishes a good one made on a moving day. Matching to the same hour is more work and it is the only version your treasury function would accept.

The summary page carries four things: the month's total in basis points and in pounds, the three pairs it concentrates in, the single worst conversion, and the same figure annualised on your own volume with the arithmetic shown rather than asserted.
