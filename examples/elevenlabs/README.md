# ElevenLabs, six target companies

**The advertiser:** ElevenLabs sells voice agents that answer a phone line or a support thread.

**The run:** layer 2, a demonstration. Built as drafts, never activated. This is the rebuild
of 23 September 2026, after a dry run twelve days earlier was rejected. The research and the
committee carried over; the pages, the films and the cards were rebuilt.

## How the list was built

Every organisation here is required to publish how badly it answers. Ofcom prints average
wait and abandonment for telecoms. The FCA complaints return names volumes and causes. The
CAA counts escalated disputes. HMRC publishes its own performance against target. **The
number that shames them is a number they have to file.**

| Account | The published figure that put them on the list | Reachable |
|---|---|---|
| [BT](accounts/bt/) | 3:33 average wait, one caller in seven gone before an adviser picked up | 800 |
| [British Gas](accounts/british-gas/) | 120,156 complaints in Q2, same-day resolution down from 54.2% to 42.5% in one quarter | 820 |
| [HMRC](accounts/hmrc/) | a floor of 11 minutes 35 seconds, and 12.1% who never get through | 890 |
| [easyJet](accounts/easyjet/) | a help line published as open 8am to 8pm, and 2,315 escalated disputes in a quarter | 800 |
| [Admiral](accounts/admiral/) | 65,577 complaints, main cause named as customer service and errors | 710 |
| [Monzo](accounts/monzo/) | 67,488 complaints in six months, 64% taking longer than three days | 460 |

## The finding: one lever does not fit six accounts

Applied as a single campaign-wide setting, the narrowing left **every one of the six above
band**: BT at 12,000, HMRC at 7,000, easyJet at 3,700, British Gas at 3,300, Admiral at
2,100, Monzo at 1,800. LinkedIn wants 300 to 1,000 for a one-to-one ad set.

Each account needed its own lever. Four took five functions at Manager and above. HMRC needed
Director and above, because it is large enough that Manager still returns thousands. **BT
needed a list of 38 named committee titles**, because no function filter cut it finely enough.
The lever is chosen per account, and the per-account choice is on each ad sheet.

## One invented figure per page, and why it is there

Each page draws the object this reader looks at every day: a wallboard, a departures board, a
renewal letter, a hold message. Every tile on it carries a published figure, **except one**.

That one is the outcome being sold, drawn on the board so it can be seen against what is there
now. It is captioned on the page, *"The lit line is not there yet. The rest is."*, and named
again in the footer. These footers are the only ones in this repository that point at their
own fabricated element.

It is a deliberate choice and worth arguing with. The Serval run's log records invented
figures being found and stripped from all seven cards. The difference is that those were
accidents and unlabelled; this is one figure, captioned twice, and it is the offer itself.
