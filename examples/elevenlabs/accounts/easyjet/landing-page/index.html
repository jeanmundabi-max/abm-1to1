Why easyJet can say yes to the first step without a procurement

A regulated firm cannot let a third party’s software talk to its customers because someone filled in a web form. That is a conduct decision with rules attached, and rightly so. It cannot hand customer data to an unappointed vendor, and it cannot let a vendor into its telephony on a demonstration.

None of those things is being asked. The first exchange is a phone number, travelling in one direction, built from pages easyJet published itself. Ringing a number is not a procurement, it creates no obligation, it needs no contract, and it needs no supplier record. It is the same act as listening to a voicemail somebody left you.

The practical test: what would this look like written down? Written down, it is: an outside party built a demonstration from our own public help pages and gave us a number to ring. We were not asked for anything and we gave nothing. That sentence survives a risk committee, which is the only bar that counts.
