# BRIEF — WYN × the Home Office

**Self-authored, not interviewed.** The client for this run is the operator, and the account is a
public body that was never asked. Everything below is answered in WYN's voice from what WYN and
the Home Office both publish, and it is marked so that nobody later reads it as a transcript.

## The eight answers

1. **Vibe.** Filed, ruled, unarguable. References: a laid-out award notice, a Hansard column, a
   National Audit Office table.
2. **The scroll journey.** The register first, because it is theirs. Then the finding inside it.
   Then one sentence with nothing in it. Then the comparison, held. Then the two ways in, the
   seats, the ask.
3. **The energy curve.** Quiet, rising, flat, loudest at the comparison, settling.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage.** In the table below. **The one moment:**
   It is the site where the page refuses to tell you whether being the biggest line is good.
5. **One thing no site they have seen does.** Refuse to say which of two numbers is the good one,
   while giving the reader the instrument to look.
6. **Distance from premium minimal.** Editorial, close to a printed document. Not brutalist and
   never playful: the reader is signing public money.
7. **One world or distinct scenes?** One world. The register is a single artefact and the page is
   that artefact, ruled.
8. **Assets already held.** The account's own award notices, read from Contracts Finder into
   `00-inputs/contracts_live.csv`. No generated footage, no stock, no spend.

## The finding this page exists for

The Home Office sits at the top of two supplier lists that are nothing like each other. On Amazon Web Services there are eight other public buyers in the country; on Phoenix Software there are seventy-six. The page is built on that asymmetry, because it changes what a comparison can honestly say.

## The feeling curve, written before the acts

| Act | Feeling | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Recognition | their own eight hundred million pounds of term value, drawn |
| The two at the top | Unease | two suppliers, and only one of them is comparable at depth |
| The hinge | Understanding | one sentence on white, nothing else |
| The same day | The peak | the handle, and a page that refuses to say which figure is better |
| Two ways in | Relief | the route that asks for nothing, leading |
| The seats | Permission | three first questions, and one named seat with the reason the others are not |
| The published read | Calm | the rules converge into the rule above the form |

**The peak is act 4** and it carries the largest span on the page, 3.0 against a mean of 1.7.
The act before it is the quietest thing on the page, deliberately.

## Authored silence

Act 3 is one sentence, no figure, no table, no accent and no ask. It is not dead scroll. It is
there because act 2 hands the reader a number and act 4 asks them to work, and without a beat
between them the second one lands on a full mind.

## The hook, and where it lives

**The biggest line on both lists.** It is the first line of the film, the headline of the ad card, and it is not repeated
in the page's own h1, because the film and the h1 sit in the same viewport.
