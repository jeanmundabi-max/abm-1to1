# SCORE — WYN × HM Revenue & Customs

## Page grammar: RULED LEDGER

Chosen over filmic one-shot, split stage, printed chapters, live surface, worldflight,
brutalist wall and gallery.

Why the other seven lost. **Split stage** is the Revolut grammar and reusing it would make the
second client look like the first, which is the exact failure this build exists to correct.
**Filmic one-shot** is what every prior build reached for. **Live surface** implies data the
operator does not have. **Worldflight** needs generated footage and a budget, and this audience
would read the spend as the thing they are being sold. **Printed chapters** was the near miss and
Ruled Ledger is its stricter cousin: the page IS the artefact the argument is about, a public
register, so the chrome is a ruled margin and a running left rule rather than chapter furniture.

- **Nav:** none. A running hairline down the left margin with the act's date range.
- **Hero:** the film held in the upper band, the register drawn beneath it.
- **Close:** the rules converge into one, and the form sits on it.

## Signature move: THE HELD VERDICT

Two rails of identical stroke weight and identical type size carry HMRC's annual figure and the
Home Office's. The reader drags a handle along the shared date axis and both rails travel
together. **Whatever the reader does, the page never marks one as better.** No colour, no arrow,
no label. The accent belongs to the date and to nothing else, ever.

Coded in the page off `--sc-p` and a pointer handler. The engine is untouched.

## The film, directed by the council

Four directors pitched in parallel: Apple, Super Bowl Spectacle, McDonald's, Beats by Dre.

**They converged without speaking to each other**: all four put the accent on the DATE and not
on the money, which is the only way to honour the rule that the direction is never asserted.

**Chosen: THE RAIL, McDonald's**, on the grounds that one hairline a five year old could redraw
carries the whole argument, and the argument itself is a line: a term runs, ends, and is settled
again. Grafted in:

- from **Beats**, purple touches one object only for the whole film, the renewal bar, and the
  card is the freeze at the frame the accent lands;
- from **Super Bowl**, the rows align **on the date, not on the money**, and there are no cuts,
  because a cut is where a thing gets hidden and this reader is trained to notice;
- from **Apple**, every figure carries its source permanently at 20% ink: *HMRC, Contracts
  Finder*. We cite them to themselves.

**Rejected: a countdown, in every pitch.** A rendered film cannot recompute itself. The live WYN
page hardcoded "ends in 101 days" on 21 August and was wrong the next morning.

| t | On screen | Motion |
|---|---|---|

| 16.0 | Nine contracts. Eight buyers. | The rail splits into two parallel rails |
| 20.0 | HMRC £116,746,575 · Home Office £150,196,594 | Both rails travel, identical weight |
| 24.0 | **30 November 2026** | Both halt on the same tick. **The only accent, on the date** |
| 28.0 | One document. Public data. No fee. | The rails converge into one and the offer sets on it |

**The hook**, on screen at 0.0 and the first line of the ad body: *A framework caps. A call-off
pays.* Six words. A truth hook, not a tease: a finance director in a department reads it and
thinks that is exactly right, and most people here do not know it.

**The belief shift:** the framework is not the price. The call-off is, and the renewal is the
only moment it moves.

### Dwell check

Open loop set at 0.0 and unresolved until 24.0. No static frame: the rail travels at one
unbroken speed for all thirty seconds and type is uncovered by the travel rather than faded in.
Loop friendly: frame 900 leaves the rail mid-travel, so a replay reads as continuous.
