The one in purple

Amazon Web Services EMEA SARL, UK Branch. £350,000,000 over the term, £116,746,575 a year, ending 30 November 2026. It is both the largest and the first to come up, and those two facts together are the only reason this page exists.

The shape of the register is the argument. Contracts do not come up evenly, they cluster, and the cluster was set years earlier by whoever signed them. 15 of your 16 live contracts are call-offs from a framework agreement, so the route was decided once and the price is decided again at every renewal.

Nothing here was obtained by request or by inference. Contracts Finder is a publication duty, the notices were filed by HMRC, and the only work done was reading them and putting them in date order.

A framework sets what a supplier may charge at most. A call-off sets what you actually pay. A renewal is the only moment that number moves.

After it renews it is settled for the whole of the next term. That is why this page carries a date rather than a season.

What it costs to do nothing

Nothing, on the day. That is what makes it easy. The cost is that a number agreed on 30 November 2026 is then the number for the whole next term.

On a call-off running at £116,746,575 a year, one percent is £1,167,466 a year and two percent is £2,334,932. This page is not claiming either applies to you. It is showing what the arithmetic of one percent looks like at this scale, so that reading a free document before a signature is easy to justify.

The same arithmetic runs on the other 15 contracts, £77,556,727 a year between them, spread across the next 7 months.

There is a second cost and it is harder to see. A renewal agreed without a comparison is a renewal nobody can defend afterwards. If a public accounts committee or an auditor later sets your call-off beside another department's, the useful answer is not that the price was reasonable. It is that it was checked, and here is what against. That document exists before the signature or it does not exist at all.

What is actually in the document

What arrives

A single PDF. One page per contract, and a summary sheet in front of it. For each of your 16 live contracts it names the supplier, the published value, the term, the annual value, the end date, and what the same supplier is charging other departments on comparable published work. Where there is no comparator it says so rather than estimating.

What does not arrive

A savings figure with your name on it. Nobody outside your building knows your consumption, your scope, or what sits behind a framework call-off, and a number produced without those is theatre. The document says where you sit and stops.

Why a civil servant can accept this without asking anyone

This is the question that decides whether anything else on the page matters, so it gets its own answer rather than a line in a list.

A government department cannot engage a commercial party on a share of savings because someone filled in a web form. That is a procurement decision with rules attached, and rightly so. It also cannot hand a live supplier proposal to an unappointed third party, because that proposal is commercial in confidence and the department holds it on terms.

Neither of those things is being asked. The first exchange is one document, travelling in one direction, built from award notices HM Revenue & Customs published itself under a publication duty. Receiving a document is not a procurement, it does not create an obligation, it does not need a contract, and it does not need a supplier record. It is the same act as reading a report somebody sent you.

The practical test civil servants apply is a good one: what would this look like written down in a submission? Written down, it is: an outside party compiled our own published contract notices, set them beside other departments' published notices for the same suppliers, and sent us the comparison. We were not asked for anything and we gave nothing. That sentence survives a permanent secretary reading it, which is the only bar that counts.

Everything past the first document changes shape, and the page says so rather than letting it blur. The negotiation work is a commercial arrangement, it goes through your commercial team, and it starts with them and with information assurance rather than with anybody on this page. It may never happen, and the document is useful either way.
