01Who we are targeting, and how the list was built

The list was not bought and it was not guessed. A company that has the problem Serval fixes is running a ticket queue today, and a company running ServiceNow or Jira Service Management publishes that fact without meaning to: the instance answers on the open internet, at the company's own name. That is what put each company on this list, and it was true on the morning it was checked rather than in a file of unknown age.

4possible ways of detecting a service desk were tested
2survived, because the other two also answered for a name that does not exist
20companies built on the two that survived, across fifteen sectors
7taken forward, after every figure was read again on the day
0figures bought from a data vendor, on any page or any ad

The two checks, and the control that makes them mean anything

Both checks are free, both take a second, and anyone can repeat them. What makes them evidence rather than a trick is the third line: a made-up company name, run through the same check. If a check answers for a name that does not exist, it proves nothing about anybody.

The checkWhat a real company returnsWhat the made-up name returns
Its ServiceNow instancean addressnothing at all
Its Jira Service Management portala redirect to the service desk logina 404

Two checks were thrown away for failing exactly that test. A plain lookup of company.atlassian.net answers for any name on earth, including the made-up one. And hostnames like it. or helpdesk. resolve at companies that answer every subdomain, so they say nothing about whether a help desk is there. Both were dropped before a single company was written down, which is why the list is 7 companies and not two hundred.

What the list is not

It is not a list of companies who want to buy something. Nobody can know that from outside. It is a list of companies who are demonstrably running the thing Serval replaces, which is the nearest honest substitute and the only one that can be checked by the person reading this page.

One finding worth more than the list

Five companies that passed every other test were dropped for the same reason: between 1,500 and 3,000 US staff, and not one of them could field 300 reachable people in an IT function. LinkedIn will not run an ad set below 300. On this channel Serval's one-to-one universe starts at roughly 8,000 US employees. Below that the ads cannot be delivered at all, however good the fit, and that is a fact about the channel rather than about those companies.

04Who sees the ad, and who does not

Each ad is shown to one organisation only. LinkedIn will not run an audience below 300 people, so the filters have to leave enough of an organisation in.

  • Only people who work at that organisationThis is the whole point of a one-to-one campaign. The ad for one department is never shown to another.
  • United States onlyLarge organisations have people abroad who are not in the US IT organisation these ads are about.
  • Information technology rolesThe buyer for this owns the internal ticket queue. LinkedIn's Information Technology function is broad and also contains engineers who never touch a service desk, so it holds people the offer does not concern. That is deliberate: the ad names that company's own service desk and its own filed headcount, so the people it does not concern will scroll past it.
  • Junior roles are excludedInterns, trainees and entry level. They are not on the buying committee and including them would spend the budget on impressions that cannot act.

05How a visit gets traced back to the ad

When somebody clicks one of these ads, three short labels are added to the end of the web address. Your analytics reads them and can then say where that visitor came from. Nothing is installed on your site and nothing is collected about the person. It is a label on the visit, not a tag on the visitor.

https://jeanmundabi-max.github.io/mdb-abm/serval/wayfair/?source=linkedin&medium=paid-social&campaign=serval-esm-1to1-2026-09
sourceWhich platform sent them. Here, always LinkedIn.
mediumPaid or not. This separates ads from a post someone shared.
campaignWhich campaign. Lets you compare this against anything else you run.
There is no per-account label on these ad sets, and that is a gap, not a choice. Every one of the seven ad sets carries the same labels, so a visit from one is indistinguishable from a visit from another and you cannot tell which account is reacting. Adding one label per ad set fixes it, changes nothing else, and is worth doing before anything runs.
These labels are not named the way analytics tools expect. Google Analytics and most other tools only recognise a label when its name begins with utm_. The names above do not, so the visits will arrive but will not be grouped as campaign traffic. Renaming them is a settings change on each ad set. It creates nothing and it can be done before launch.

Two honest limits. This shows a visit arriving, not what the visitor did next, which is the separate piece of tracking that is not yet in place. And this naming was chosen by us, because there is no existing campaign on the account to copy a convention from. If you already use a different naming scheme elsewhere, yours should win and these are quick to change.

06Where this sits, and what comes after it

A campaign like this has three stages and this is the first one. Each stage has a different job, a different audience and a different way of being judged. Getting them in the wrong order is the usual reason paid campaigns disappoint.

Stage one, coldthis is what is built

Nobody in these organisations has heard of you. The job of this stage is to be recognised and to build the list of people who reacted. It is deliberately not trying to make anyone buy. The ads are set to optimise for engagement rather than for visits, because asking LinkedIn to buy clicks from people with no reason to be interested spends the budget on the wrong clicks.

Judge this stage on how many of the right people saw it and reacted, not on sales.

Stage two, warmnot built

This is where the first stage pays off. The people who reacted are now a much smaller and much warmer audience, and they get different ads: proof, a case study, a worked example. Only here does it become worth paying for a visit to the site.

The important part is who counts as having reacted. It is not only the people who clicked.

Watched the videoHalf of it or more
Visited the siteAny page, from any source
Reacted to the adA like, a comment, an expand

Three separate groups, and two of them never clicked anything. Someone who watched thirty seconds of a video and moved on is a warmer contact than most people who click, and they can be followed up.

Stage three, readynot built

The smallest group: people who watched nearly all of a video, or visited the pages that only a serious buyer visits. These get a direct ask, and this is the only stage judged on meetings booked.

The reason for the order: only about one in twenty organisations is ready to buy at any given moment, and most of those prefer a name they already know. The first stage exists so that when the service desk contract does come up for renewal, you are already a name they recognise rather than a cold email.

07Ads are one channel, not the campaign

The line that is easiest to get wrong is the first four weeks. Nobody is contacted in that window, deliberately. Reaching out to an organisation that has only just seen your name is a cold approach with extra steps, and it burns the account. The waiting is the part that makes the later contact land.

When somebody is contacted, what they reacted to decides what is said. A company that read the page about running two service desks at once gets a different opening line from one that only watched a video. That is the whole reason for running one ad per account rather than one ad for everybody. One rule holds throughout: the ad is never mentioned. You reference the subject they showed interest in, never the fact that you watched them click something.

One risk worth knowing now

LinkedIn hides engagement figures when too few people at an organisation have reacted, as a privacy measure. On a campaign aimed at one organisation at a time, that threshold is close to the volumes involved, so the first weeks may show less detail than expected.

The way around it is to read the figures over a month rather than week by week, which is how this will be reported. It is worth saying in advance rather than explaining later.

08Done, and not done

Side by side, because a handover that only lists successes is a sales document.

Done

  • Seven accounts chosen from published evidenceEach one traces to a named service desk that answered a public request on the day, with the address it answered on.
  • One page and one ad per accountNothing is shared between them. Each argument is a different company's own filing and a different service desk.
  • The audience narrowed and measuredMeasured again after every change to the filters, so the figures on this page are current.
  • Click tracking labels are on every ad setA visit from these ads carries labels. Section 05 says what still has to change about them before your analytics will group them as campaign traffic.
  • Audience expansion and the off-site network switched offBoth are on by default and both would show the ads to people outside the seven organisations.

Not done

  • Nothing activated, nothing spentEvery ad set is a draft. Going live is one deliberate action that has not been taken.
  • Conversion tracking is not in placeWe can see a visit arrive. We cannot yet see what that visitor did next, which needs a tag on your site and a decision from you about what counts as a result.
  • Stage two cannot run yetFollowing up with the people who reacted needs a separate LinkedIn permission on this account. Worth requesting now, because it takes weeks and the first stage is building that list from day one.
  • The named buyers are incompletePublic records name the senior people at some of these organisations and not others.
  • The daily budget was assumedA figure is set on each ad set so it is ready to run. It is a placeholder until you confirm it.
  • The click labels are not finishedThey are set, but not named the way analytics tools read, and there is no label that differs per account. Section 05 has the detail. Fixing it is a settings change and creates nothing.

09What we need from you before anything runs

Four decisions. None of them are large, and all four block something specific.

  • Confirm the daily budgetBlocks activation. The figure currently set is a placeholder, not a recommendation.
  • Say what counts as a result on your siteBlocks conversion tracking. A form, a demo request, a download. Once it is named it can be measured.
  • Request the follow-up permission from LinkedInBlocks stage two. It takes weeks, so it should be requested before stage one starts rather than after.
  • Check the seven accounts are ones you want to be seen byBlocks nothing technically, but these ads name a real company's own service desk to its real staff and that should be a deliberate choice.
