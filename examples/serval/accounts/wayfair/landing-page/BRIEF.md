# BRIEF: Wayfair, for Serval

**Reader.** The person at Wayfair who owns the internal ticket queue. Not the CTO. The
Director or Senior Manager of IT Operations who opens the queue at 08:00 and is judged on
how fast it empties.

**How far from premium minimal.** Not minimal. Dense and instrument-like, closer to
editorial. Quiet is a choice that has to be argued, and there is no argument for it here:
this reader is being asked to believe that a machine can close their tickets, and a pale
page reads as a claim with nothing behind it.

**The world.** Wayfair's own purple, #7b189f, is the ground. Serval's violet, #703fdc, is
the only element that carries meaning: it marks every place where a human is currently
required and would stop being required. The colour is the argument, not the paint.

**The signature move.** Wayfair runs two service desks at once. Press and hold to run both
probes and watch both answer. Nobody at Wayfair has seen those two facts side by side.

**The number they supply.** We do not know Wayfair's monthly ticket volume and will not
invent it. The reader sets requests per employee per month on a dial, and the page does the
arithmetic against their own filed headcount. The only assumption on the page is theirs.

**Respite.** One line after the arithmetic, before the peers.

**What is inferred.** The offer. Serval publishes no pricing and no trial terms. The offer
shape is the operator's inference from Serval's published customer results, disclosed in
the footer and never on the button.
